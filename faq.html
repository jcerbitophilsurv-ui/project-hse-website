How It Works

From consultation to clean power

A straightforward path, every step guided by our team.

01

Site Assessment

A 45-minute visit. We measure the roof, look at how the sun tracks across it, and photograph your meter.

02

Custom System Design

Our engineers size the system to what you actually use, how your roof faces, and how much of your bill you want to cover.

03

Installation

Two to three days on the roof. Nothing starts until a licensed Civil Engineer has cleared the structure, and a Professional Electrical Engineer supervises the work throughout.

04

Monitoring & Warranty

A 25-year performance warranty on the panels: the manufacturer guarantees they are still producing at a stated level in year 25. A 10-year inverter warranty, and a 1-year workmanship warranty on our installation. The panels also carry a separate product warranty against defects, named in your proposal. Whatever it covers, you deal with us. We monitor your system's performance through the first year, and after that we're still the people who know your system — call us any time it's running.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions, answered

There isn't one price. Two homes with the same electricity bill can need very different systems — roof structure, shading, and how much of your usage you want to cover all change it.

What we can be exact about is what a quote includes: panels, inverter, mounting, wiring, the electrical permit and the utility application. Roof reinforcement, long cable runs or second-storey access can add to that, and we'll tell you before you decide if any apply to your home. Battery backup is a separate system, quoted on its own.

For a figure based on your own bill, use our estimator.

Installation takes 2–3 days, once approvals are in place and the roof is ready for mounting.

The overall timeline depends on your location. Some local governments and subdivisions require permits or homeowners' association approval; others do not. We confirm the requirements during the site assessment and process them on your behalf.

Panels run on daylight, not direct sun, so they keep generating through cloud and rain — just less. Heavy overcast gives roughly 10–25% of full output; light cloud is nearer 50–70%. We size systems around a full year of real weather rather than a run of clear days, so a wet week doesn't put you behind the estimate — you simply draw a little more from the grid that month.

Yes. Your first year is fully covered — we track how your system is performing, clean the panels and check the equipment. After that we're still the people who know your system: call us any time it's running, for any question about how it's working.

Solar panels on their own will not power your home during an outage. A battery will.

When the power goes out, any system connected to your electricity supply switches off automatically; it's a safety rule, so your roof isn't feeding a line someone may be working on. Add a battery and you keep power — and you decide how much it covers, from the essentials to the whole house.

It depends on where you are. A Department of Energy circular issued in August 2026 (DC2026-08-0017) bars distribution utilities from requiring pre-installation clearances, technical permits or inspection fees for systems that don't export to the grid. Local governments set their own requirements separately, and some still require an electrical permit either way. We confirm what applies to your address during the site assessment and handle it from there.

A licensed Civil Engineer checks your roof structure before we start, and how we mount depends on the roof you have. If it needs reinforcing, we tell you at the assessment.

We specify panels and inverters with a long international track record that also hold up in Philippine conditions — sustained heat, humidity and typhoon-season wind are harder on equipment than most of the climates it's designed for. The warranty is ours to honour.
